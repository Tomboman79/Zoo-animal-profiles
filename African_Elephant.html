African Elephant

The largest land animal in the planet

The African Bush Elephant, also known as the Loxodonta Africana is the largest land animal in the world, measuring up to 157 inches (400 cm), they are also known for having curved and large tusks, as well as sail like ears, they live in woodlands, grasslands, and shrublands in Africa.


Status: Endangered

Diet of the African Bush Elephant

The African Bush Elephant is a herbivore, meaning, they feed solely on plants and other non meaty foods such as:

  • Grass
  • Leaves
  • Twigs
  • Tree bark
  • Roots
  • Fruits
  • Flowers

Habitat of the African Bush Elephant

As mentioned before, the African Bush Elephant is capable of living in several ecosystems, such as:

Woodlands
Areas with scattered trees and open spaces, usually found in mild to warm climates with moderate rainfall.
Grasslands
Large open regions dominated by grasses, typically with warm summers, cold winters, and moderate rainfall.
Shrublands
Dry or semi-dry ecosystems characterized by low-growing woody shrubs and occasional small trees. They often have hot, dry summers and mild, wetter winters.

Behaviour of the African Bush Elephants

The African Bush Elephants are social animals, meaning they spend most of their lifes, if not all of it, in packs of around 5 to 25 members, but ocassionally, they can join with other packs of elephants and reach up to 500 members or even more!

Communication

African Bush Elephants communicate in many ways, like making gestures or smelling, some ways these elephants communicate are:

  • Sounds
  • Physical gestures
  • Tactile communication
  • Chemical communication (smells)

Mating

The mating rituals for African Bush Elephants consists of several things, such as displays of dominance and increased agression, but they also start secreting an odor that attracts females in order to mate with them

Predators of the African Bush Elephant

African Bush Elephants, despite their size, still have natural predators which, from most dangerous to least dangerous, are:

  1. Lions
  2. Crocodiles
  3. Spotted Hyenas
