Nurse Shark

A docile and beautiful aquatic animal

The Nurse Sharks, also known by their scientific name, Ginglymostoma Cirratum, is a very interesting shark, being really docile and being a suction feeder, their size ranges from 8.2 ft (2.50 meters) to 14 ft (4.2 meters), some of their distinguishing features include their flat bodies, their nasal barbels, and their dorsal fins, they are normally found in shallow waters around coasts ranging from the americas to Europe's borders with the Atlantic Ocean.


Status: Vulnerable

Diet of the Nurse Shark

As Carnivores, the Nurse Sharks need to find small animals they are capable of eating, along with their nature as suction feeders, their diet is almost exlusively:

  • Crustaceans
  • Small stingrays
  • Sea urchins
  • Bony fishes

Habitat of the Nurse Shark

The Nurse Shark is found mainly in coral reefs, in shallow water in depths of up to 165 ft (50 meters), commonly close to the equator and in the Atlantic Ocean.

Behaviour of the Nurse Shark

The Nurse Shark is a social animal, it is not uncommon finding them in groups of 3 to 30 specimens, thought it is easier to do so during the night, due to them being nocturnal animals.

Communication.

Nurse Sharks communicate in a variety of ways, such as:

  • Gesturing
  • Touching
  • Making sounds
  • By detecting chemicals

Mating.

Nurse Sharks perform seasonal mating, meaning, there is a specific period of time during the year were Nurse Sharks have babies, during these times, the sharks can lay upwards of 20 eggs at a time, being polygynandrous just makes sure there are more babies at the end of the month.

polygynandrous: being able to have more than one mate at a time

Predators of the Nurse Shark

While the Nurse Shark does not have any active predator, since they normally are the biggest carnivore in the area, remains of these sharks have still been found in other animals, particularly bigger sharks, such as the tiger shark.
